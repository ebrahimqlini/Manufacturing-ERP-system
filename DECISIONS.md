# Architecture decisions

## Recorded baseline

- Use Django 5.2 LTS with Python 3.12 and PostgreSQL 18.6. Python 3.12.15 was the recommended patch at foundation selection, but the exact micro version is not enforced; the project `.venv` was verified with Python 3.12.14. Prefer the latest supported micro release when setting up or deploying. Django's compatibility documentation lists Python 3.12 for Django 5.2 and marks 5.2 as LTS. PostgreSQL lists major 18 as supported through November 2030. The manifest pins Django 5.2.18, Psycopg 3.3.6, and resolved runtime dependencies; Python and PostgreSQL server versions are runtimes, not Python packages.
- Use Django templates for the initial UI. The starter page declares Arabic and RTL; Django localization is enabled and both Arabic and English are configured.
- Use PostgreSQL for deployment. SQLite is only a low-friction local/test default; PostgreSQL-specific behavior must be checked against PostgreSQL before release.
- Read database settings from environment variables. Keep local `.env` ignored and commit only a placeholder example.
- Serve Bootstrap RTL 5.3.8 from the repository's static files, with its license, to avoid a runtime CDN dependency. Keep Arabic content RTL and isolate mixed-direction identifiers explicitly (for example with `<bdi dir="ltr">` for Latin product codes).
- Use `Decimal`/`DecimalField` for money and exact inventory quantities. Never introduce floats into financial or stock calculations. Prompt 1 stores quantities/conversion factors at 12 fractional places and rejects overflow without rounding; future business rounding policies still require owner approval.
- Introduce domain apps incrementally. Do not model presumed factory policies in the foundation.

## Expanded factory scope

The factory has two production lines sharing the same ERP support modules:

1. **Paper cups:** the known process may include printing, cutting, cup forming, bottom assembly where applicable, and packaging. Product/routing configuration must determine which operations apply.
2. **Paper-fiber food containers/trays:** the confirmed process fact is pressing fiber material in a mold. Do not infer drying, pulp production, heating, trimming, other stages, raw-material specifications, machinery, units, or production rates. Represent it as configurable product-specific BOMs and routings; the confirmed operation candidate is seeded, but no product recipe or complete route is assumed.

Both lines use one shared product catalog, warehouse/location structure, stock ledger, purchasing and supplier records, customer/sales flows, chart of accounts, journals, and reporting layer. Do not create parallel inventory, purchasing, sales, or accounting subsystems per line. Production orders and attributable costs carry a production-line dimension; reports support both consolidated factory totals and line-level views. Shared inputs remain shared inventory and are attributed to a line through the related transaction, not through duplicate stock balances.

Prompt 1 established configurable products/categories and units of measure plus shared warehouses, locations, and stock movements. Initial category coverage includes raw materials, work in progress, finished goods, packaging materials, scrap, and consumables. Prompt 2 adds configurable, versioned BOM/routing and production-order records for both lines without hardcoding unconfirmed factory details. Prompt 2 does not create WIP inventory balances. A later monthly product-costing step (see "Production costing decisions") calculates per-product costs from actual production and expense data while still avoiding any WIP balance or accounting posting. Later manufacturing/accounting work must attribute approved production costs by line without creating duplicate ledgers. Detailed boundaries and acceptance tests are in [PROMPT_SPECS.md](PROMPT_SPECS.md).

## Prompt 1 implementation decisions

- The shared catalog uses a globally unique SKU. Categories are configurable and seeded with shared stock categories; category membership is not production-line identity.
- Unit dimensions are configurable keys. Alternate conversions belong to a product and express base units per alternate unit. Quantity and conversion factors are stored as `Decimal` with 12 fractional places. Conversions that need more precision are rejected; no rounding is applied.
- The inventory ledger stores immutable movement headers plus signed per-location entries. Receipts, issues, adjustments, and openings create one leg; transfers create both legs in one database transaction. Corrections create linked reversal movements. A reversal movement is terminal and cannot itself be reversed; this keeps the audit chain unambiguous and prevents stock from being silently re-issued. A unique reference is required for each posted movement.
- Warehouse/location balances are computed by aggregating ledger entries; there is no editable quantity on the product. Auxiliary per-item/location lock rows serialize concurrent postings on PostgreSQL and do not store balances.
- Opening balance is staff-only, allowed only before that item has any ledger activity in the selected location, and cannot be repeated there. Adjustment and reversal reasons are mandatory.
- Confirmed factory policy: physical inventory must never become negative. The inventory posting service validates ledger balances inside its transaction after locking each product/location. Production orders may be released with a material shortage; manufacturing tracks planned, issued, and remaining quantities and permits valid partial issues over time.
- PostgreSQL triggers reject UPDATE/DELETE of posted movement and ledger rows, and guarded Django querysets reject normal ORM update/delete/bulk_update calls. These protections do not defend against a database owner disabling triggers, TRUNCATE, or unauthorized direct INSERTs; production database privileges and every writer still require control.
- Partial future production material issue is explicitly allowed: planned requirement, issued-to-date, and outstanding quantity are separate facts. If 120 kg is required and 100 kg is available, a valid 100 kg issue may be posted and 20 kg remains outstanding for a later issue; shortage itself never posts stock.
- Movements expose an optional generic `(source_reference_type, source_reference_id)` pair so future apps can identify their source without a foreign key or import from a not-yet-implemented domain app. When supplied, the pair is unique and represents one source posting event/line, protecting retries from duplicate ledger effects.
- Lot/serial tracking and inventory valuation are not implemented. No production-line identifier is added to shared stock; later manufacturing events can reference this shared service through explicit source references.

## Production costing decisions

- No inventory valuation method is approved and the shared stock ledger carries no unit cost, so monthly costing does not use FIFO or weighted average. Instead it prices actual material issues with owner-entered `RawMaterialPrice` rows and labels the result a provisional standard until an accountant approves a valuation method. The screen shows a "provisional until approved" banner, and a missing or future-dated price is reported instead of being silently reused.
- Material consumption is counted strictly from actual stock movements dated within the calendar month, excluding reversed issues. It is never taken from BOM plan quantities, and each movement is counted once.
- Electricity and water are treated as required utilities for any month with production. A missing utility bill (no record for that month) marks the period incomplete with an explicit note and blocks finalization; it is never assumed to be zero. A deliberately recorded `0.00` is a confirmed zero-cost bill and is stored and shown as such, so it is distinguishable from an unrecorded bill.
- Labor is also treated as a required monthly operating record whenever production exists, for the same reason: an unrecorded labor row must not be silently treated as zero. `CostingPeriod.labor_recorded` distinguishes a missing labor row (period incomplete and blocked from finalization) from an explicitly recorded `0.00` (a confirmed zero). The API exposes this flag and the missing-data notes.
- Each period carries explicit `start_date`/`end_date` calendar boundaries, set from its year/month on every calculation (existing rows backfilled by migration `0005`). Material issues and production results are attributed by those boundaries, so one movement is never counted in two periods and recalculating one month leaves other months untouched.
- Utility bills are attributed wholly to the operator-selected month. The model records explicitly that a bill's period may not match the consumption month it covers; because the existing data model has no bill-period fields, bill-vs-consumption timing is documented as a known attribution limitation rather than silently re-based on a new accounting rule.
- Operating expenses (labor / electricity / water / other industrial) are recorded per month with a scope of factory, production line, or product. Line- and product-scoped amounts are attributed directly; factory-wide amounts are allocated by the period's selected policy (units produced, material cost, material weight, or equal-per-line then units). Allocation always reconciles to the recorded bucket total to the cent, so no expense is duplicated or lost across products or periods.
- A scoped expense whose line/product has no matching production is allocated through an explicit fallback, but that fallback is a review state, not a clean result. The period is marked incomplete, the affected product entries are `INCOMPLETE`, and finalization is blocked. A product with zero good units likewise has no usable good-unit cost and cannot finalize silently. The API reads these persisted statuses rather than recomputing the allocation.
- Multiple `MonthlyExpense` rows for the same month/type/scope are allowed and summed, because a month can legitimately contain more than one bill, payroll entry, or industrial charge. No unique per-type constraint or automatic duplicate detector is imposed; descriptions/notes remain the operator control, and the period review/finalization step is the safeguard. This limitation is retained rather than guessing which equal-looking rows are accidental.
- The waste policy affects the computed numbers, not just a stored flag. `ABSORBED_NORMAL` loads the full material cost on good units; `SEPARATE_ABNORMAL` splits material cost between good output and recorded scrap in proportion to recorded quantities, displayed separately and never added on top of the material total.
- Costing output is financial data and is not exposed to every authenticated user. Every costing page that shows money (periods, breakdowns, comparison, expenses, raw-material prices, reference selling prices) is limited to staff users or to non-staff users explicitly granted the standard Django permission `costing.view_costing_financials`. That permission is never assigned automatically; a finance user receives it deliberately (for example through a group). Write actions stay staff-only, and the project adds no new role/group subsystem.
- Costing is computed bottom-up at the production-order level so order entries sum exactly to product entries and period totals. Periods are draft until a staff user finalizes them; finalization is blocked while required data (prices, utilities, allocation bases) is missing, and reopening a finalized period requires a recorded reason. Costing does not post accounting entries, selling prices are reference-only and never mixed into production cost, and no WIP balances are created.

## Purchasing decisions

- Purchasing keeps documents distinct from stock effects: a purchase order is a commercial document, and only a receipt posts to the shared inventory ledger through the existing `inventory.services.post_movement` service. No purchasing code writes stock balances or ledger rows directly.
- A purchase order lifecycle is `DRAFT → CONFIRMED`, plus `CANCELLED` from either state while no receipt exists. Draft orders are editable; confirming locks the line set. Confirming requires at least one valid line, an active supplier, active products/units, and a valid unit conversion per line. Cancelling requires a reason and is rejected once any receipt exists, so received goods keep their audit trail.
- Received and outstanding quantities are derived from receipt documents (reversed movements excluded), not stored as a mutable counter. Receiving is allowed only on confirmed orders, blocks duplicate receipt references and over-receiving per line, and rejects zero/negative quantities; the whole receipt (document, lines, and stock movements) is one transaction. Correcting a receipt is done by reversing its inventory movement, which automatically restores the outstanding quantity and the derived receipt state.
- Receiving uses a single destination location per receipt and validates each line against the product's configured unit conversions. Purchase prices are snapshotted on the receipt line (`unit_price`, `price_per_base_unit`, `line_total`) for traceability; receipt value is quantity-in-base × the snapshotted base price, rounded to cents. These snapshots never change `RawMaterialPrice` or the costing valuation method, and costing keeps using owner-entered provisional prices until an accountant approves a valuation method.
- Purchase orders and receipts carry optional supplier references and notes. Tax, payment terms, supplier invoices, and accounting postings are not implemented; they depend on the later shared-accounting phase and accountant decisions.
- Supplier records hold contact data only (code, names, contact person, phone, email, address, notes, active flag) and no bank, tax, or payment details. All purchasing pages and writes are staff-only because they expose prices; the navigation link is hidden from other users.

## Sales decisions

- Sales keeps commercial documents distinct from stock effects: a sales order is a commercial document, and only a shipment posts to the shared inventory ledger through the existing `inventory.services.post_movement` service. No sales code writes stock balances or ledger rows directly.
- A sales-order lifecycle is `DRAFT → CONFIRMED → COMPLETED`, plus `CANCELLED` from draft or confirmed while no shipment exists. Draft orders are editable; confirming locks the line set. Confirming requires at least one valid line, an active customer, active products/units, and a valid unit conversion per line. Completing requires the full ordered quantity to have been shipped (net of reversals) and at least one shipment. Cancelling requires a reason and is rejected once any shipment document exists, even if its stock movement was later reversed, so shipped goods keep their audit trail.
- Shipped and outstanding quantities are derived from shipment documents (reversed movements excluded), not stored as a mutable counter. Shipping is allowed only on confirmed orders, blocks duplicate shipment references and over-shipping per line, and rejects zero/negative quantities; the whole shipment (document, lines, and stock movements) is one transaction. Correcting a shipment is done by reversing its inventory movement, which automatically restores the outstanding quantity and the derived shipment state. A completed order remains `COMPLETED` historically; if a reversal leaves outstanding quantity, an explicit `COMPLETED → CONFIRMED` reopen is available to staff with a mandatory audit reason. The reopen locks the order and only succeeds while outstanding quantity is positive, after which the remaining quantity can be shipped and the order completed again. Completion remains a point-in-time check; a concurrent reversal may require the same explicit reopen, so the state is recoverable rather than a dead end.
- Shipping uses a single source location per shipment and validates each line against the product's configured unit conversions. Sales prices are snapshotted on the order line (`unit_price`, `price_per_base_unit`, `line_total`) and again on the shipment line for traceability. A shipment line's commercial total is always `quantity_base × price_per_base_unit`, rounded to cents, so shipping in a different compatible unit remains correct. `ShipmentLine.unit_price` is the agreed order-line unit price and is labelled as such in the shipment screens; it is never presented as a per-shipment-unit price. Document value is preserved after reversal, while net shipped value excludes reversed lines. These snapshots never change `RawMaterialPrice`, the costing valuation method, or production costs.
- Customer records hold contact data only (code, Arabic/English names, contact person, phone, email, address, notes, active flag) and no national ID, bank, tax, or payment details. All customer and sales pages and writes are staff-only because they expose prices; the navigation link is hidden from other users.
- Customer invoices, payments, tax, returns/credit notes, and accounting postings are not implemented in this phase. They depend on the later shared-accounting phase and accountant decisions. Sales totals are commercial snapshots, not accounting-grade revenue or profit recognition.

## Mobile API decisions

- The mobile boundary is a versioned REST API under `/api/v1/`, implemented with Django REST Framework. Mobile clients use the API and never connect directly to PostgreSQL. The API is an adapter over existing models/services; it does not own a second inventory, sales, purchasing, or costing ledger.
- API authentication is DRF token authentication only. Session authentication is not enabled for the API, and production use requires HTTPS. Tokens are obtained from `/api/v1/auth/token/` and revoked by `/api/v1/auth/logout/`.
- Roles are explicit and separate. A **customer** is an active non-staff user linked one-to-one to an active `sales.Customer` record. An **owner** is an active staff user, reusing the existing internal/staff boundary. Staff cannot use customer endpoints, and customers cannot use owner endpoints. No customer self-registration, password-reset, or role-assignment API is implemented.
- Customer product visibility is an explicit catalog flag (`Product.available_to_customers`), not an inference from category name. Customer responses exclude inventory balances, low-stock thresholds, internal costs, supplier data, and costing records. A customer can read only their own orders; another customer's order returns `404` to avoid disclosing existence.
- Customer prices are limited to the owner-managed reference selling price on explicitly visible products and to prices already recorded on that customer's own orders. The reference price is not a quotation. Production costs and margins are never exposed.
- Low-stock alerts use an explicit per-product threshold (`Product.low_stock_threshold`). A threshold of zero disables alerts for that product. This adds a configuration input; it does not invent an inventory valuation or reorder policy.
- Customer order requests are created only through the sales service. The service creates a `DRAFT` order with `customer_submitted=true` and zero prices pending staff review. It does not confirm the order, reserve stock, create a shipment, or post inventory. Staff must price and progress the request through the existing sales workflow. Customer order request idempotency keys are not implemented.
- A positive price on every line is the explicit staff review signal for a customer-submitted order. `confirm_order` rejects such an order while any line still has a zero price, so it stays `DRAFT`. `create_shipment` independently rejects an unpriced customer-submitted order, including a legacy/bypassed confirmed order. Customer `pricing_status` is `PENDING_REVIEW` only while a draft is unpriced, `RECORDED` when all prices are positive, and `UNPRICED` for a legacy confirmed zero-priced order so it is never falsely shown as still awaiting review. Staff order list/detail and Django admin expose the customer-submitted and pricing state.
- Owner dashboard endpoints query current persisted records on every request. They are read-only projections and do not recalculate product costs. Responses are marked `Cache-Control: no-store`; no WebSocket, push-notification, or other real-time infrastructure is introduced. Mobile clients refresh on demand or after an operation.
- Dashboard document counts and financial values have explicitly different bases. `orders_total` and `shipments_count`/`receipts_count` include cancelled or fully reversed documents; `non_cancelled_orders_count`, `cancelled_orders_count`, `active_shipments_count`/`active_receipts_count`, and `fully_reversed_shipments_count`/`fully_reversed_receipts_count` expose the active/audit split, and `count_basis` states each definition. Values continue to exclude cancelled orders and reversed lines. Production status counts use `ProductionOrder.created_at`; output uses `ProductionResult.occurred_at`; `status_counts_basis` and `output_basis` state this explicitly and the API does not pretend they are a single cohort.
- Dashboard commercial sales/purchase document values are explicitly labelled as non-accounting values. The API returns `null` for accounting profit, net profit, receivables, payables, and cash, and reports `accounting_available=false` with missing dependencies. It never computes sales minus purchases as profit. Persisted monthly costing outputs remain the source for product costs. The separate persisted monthly management result and loss carry-forward are labelled as non-accounting.
- Labor, electricity, water, and other-industrial allocation is performed by the existing monthly costing service, not by the API. The dashboard reads persisted period totals and product-entry allocation components and exposes a display-level `reconciliation` object (`materials_reconcile`, `cost_total_reconciles`) without recomputing unit costs. It explicitly records that the allocation has not been independently recalculated by the API and is not accounting-final.
- The API uses page-number pagination with a maximum page size, a stable validation-error envelope, JSON-only renderers/parsers, token authentication, request throttling, and staff-only schema/docs. The schema/docs views additionally accept a staff Django session so they are usable in a browser; the actual API endpoints remain token-only. There is no CORS policy because native mobile clients do not need one; a future Flutter web client would require an explicit policy.

## Reporting decisions

- The `reports` app is a read-only service layer plus staff-only web pages. It owns no models and writes no domain records. Both the existing owner API and the web reports consume the same functions, so there is no parallel dashboard API or duplicated sales/inventory posting logic.
- Reports read authoritative persisted records: shared inventory balances from `inventory.services.ledger_balances`, document values and net quantities from persisted sales/purchasing lines and movements, production output from `ProductionResult`, and product costs from persisted `CostingPeriod`/`ProductCostingEntry` rows. Display-level reconciliation arithmetic is allowed; report code does not recalculate product costs, allocation drivers, or ledger balances.
- Cancelled sales/purchase orders and fully reversed documents remain in audit counts but are excluded from active commercial values and quantities. Reversed movement lines are excluded from net totals and reported separately. A document is active when it has at least one non-reversed line; partial and fully reversed counts are distinct.
- Commercial order value, shipment/receipt value, and recognized revenue are separate concepts. Reports label sales and purchase document values as non-accounting; no report computes revenue, profit, receivables, payables, or cash.
- Money is formatted as `Decimal` with two-decimal currency precision. Base quantities retain twelve decimal places and are grouped by base UOM. Quantities in different dimensions are never summed. Report payloads include explicit units, date bases, included/excluded statuses, source records, completeness, and authoritative/estimate labels.
- Production can be filtered by production line because production orders carry a required line. Sales, purchasing, and shared inventory records have no duplicated per-line ledger and are not artificially allocated to lines. Production reconciliation compares total output with the sum attributed to the displayed lines and exposes any unallocated amount instead of assuming zero.
- Costing reports expose `labor_recorded`, `electricity_recorded`, and `water_recorded` snapshots so a missing utility record is distinguishable from an explicitly recorded `0.00`. Incomplete periods, fallback allocations, and missing good-unit costs are surfaced as review warnings and never presented as accounting-final.
- Report and dashboard responses are built at request time with no caching, scheduled snapshots, WebSockets, or push notifications. Current stock is not presented as a historical as-of-date balance.
- Tax, accounting journals, invoices, payments, and other missing accounting facts are not invented. If a report cannot be produced from an authoritative record, it states the limitation or returns an unavailable/null value.

## Monthly profit and loss carry-forward decisions

- Monthly profit is a management operating result, not accounting net profit. `Gross Profit = Net Sales − COGS`; `Monthly Result = Gross Profit − Labor − Electricity − Maintenance − Other Operating Expenses`; the profit remaining after loss recovery is a separate field. UI and API text must not call either figure final net profit.
- COGS covers only products actually shipped in the month. It uses the latest persisted `ProductCostingEntry.material_cost_per_good_unit` on or before the shipment month. This is a documented direct-material estimate, not full inventory valuation. Labor, electricity, water, maintenance, and other overhead are deliberately excluded from COGS so period expenses are deducted once.
- Manufactured but unsold products remain inventory and are not expensed through COGS. Sales are recognized when an active shipment exists, not when cash is collected; the project has no collection ledger. Cancelled orders and reversed shipment movements are excluded. Discounts, sales returns, and credit notes are not implemented and are not invented.
- `MonthlyExpense` records category, EGP amount, period, expense/payment dates, description, reference, creator/updater, timestamps, status, and reversal audit data. Active rows are summed once for the selected month; they are not reallocated across production lines. Multiple rows of the same type remain valid and are summed.
- Labor, electricity, maintenance, and other operating expenses must each be explicitly recorded, even as `0.00`, before finalization. A missing row is missing data and is never treated as confirmed zero. Water remains recorded for costing but is outside the agreed monthly profit calculation.
- `MAINTENANCE` is excluded from product-cost allocation and deducted only once in the monthly profit calculation. Labor is therefore not both a product cost and a period expense.
- Loss carry-forward comes from the latest finalized earlier period, not from the immediately preceding calendar month and not from the year boundary. It never resets in January and is never recorded as a new expense. A positive monthly result first covers the opening accumulated loss; only the remainder is profit after recovery.
- Finalization persists the result and closing loss, is blocked while any required expense category or COGS line is incomplete, and cannot run twice. Any later finalized month blocks finalizing or reopening an older month until the later month is reopened. Reopening requires a reason and preserves the original finalization metadata with reopen audit fields. Draft successors are recalculated so their opening loss follows a corrected finalized balance.
- Finalized periods protect expense edits, costing recalculation/reopen, new shipments in the month, shipment reversals, and deletion. Expense corrections use an audited reversal (`REVERSED` plus reason/actor/time) rather than deletion, and a correction that changes a finalized costing month requires reopening the costing period and then the later profit periods in dependency order.
- Reports and the owner API read persisted monthly results and expense/COGS detail without recalculating product costs. Missing or incomplete data is surfaced as warnings; absent months are not shown as confirmed zero.

## Service and transaction conventions

Business operations that change multiple records will live in explicit service functions. Each service will validate the requested transition, use `transaction.atomic()`, enforce idempotency/uniqueness in the database where possible, and record actor/time/source for auditability. Views handle request/response concerns only. Draft documents remain editable according to policy; posted inventory and accounting records are immutable and corrected through explicit reversal/correction documents. Specific lifecycle rules remain undecided until domain phases.

## Naming and data conventions

Use singular PascalCase model names, lowercase `snake_case` fields, explicit foreign-key deletion behavior, and named database constraints for cross-row invariants where PostgreSQL can enforce them. Keep amounts and exact quantities in `Decimal`/`DecimalField`, with field precision chosen when business ranges are known. Render Latin product identifiers in mixed-direction Arabic text in an explicit left-to-right `<bdi>` element; do not infer a product-code format or normalize identifiers without a catalog policy.

## Numbering, valuation, and backups

- Document numbering must be unique under concurrent requests; generate numbers transactionally and enforce a database uniqueness constraint. Format, scope (global/per year/per branch), and gap policy are unresolved.
- No inventory valuation method or accounting integration policy is selected. FIFO, weighted average, standard cost, and treatment of overhead require factory/accountant decisions before implementation.
- Use reviewed, versioned Django migrations. Establish scheduled PostgreSQL backups, retention, encryption, off-machine copies, and restore drills before production; exact schedule and retention require owner approval.
- Run the application with a dedicated non-superuser database role. Keep migration/test privileges separate where practical; do not configure the normal application to use PostgreSQL's `postgres` superuser.

## Open factory/accounting decisions

No tax treatment, currency policy, fiscal calendar, chart of accounts, posting rules, rounding policy, stock costing method, BOM quantities/yield, production rates, scrap treatment, quality acceptance thresholds, or document-number formats are assumed. For fiber containers/trays, only pressing fiber material in a mold is confirmed; materials, machinery, units, other stages, and rates must be confirmed before configuring product data. Obtain and record the factory/accountant's decisions in the corresponding domain phase before implementing behavior. Confirm local tax/legal requirements with a qualified Egyptian accountant.
